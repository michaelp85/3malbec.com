For months the house has been a flat grey rectangle. Then the frame went up, and in a few days it turned into rooms, doorways and a roofline you can stand under.

## The numbers

- **103 m** of external wall frame
- **~105 m** of internal walls
- **2.9 m** ceilings throughout
- **340 m²** under roof

Walking the slab with chalk lines is one thing. Walking through actual doorways is something else.

## Why 140mm LVL frame

We went with an 140mm LVL frame. It's straight, it stays straight, and it doesn't twist or cup as it dries out the way sawn pine can. With 2.9 m walls and a lot of big glazing, straight matters. A frame that's out of plumb or out of square causes problems for every trade after it: plasterers, cabinetmakers, and above all the window installer. The use of LVLs, increase to 140mm and straighness requirements added around $4k extra, Paying a bit extra now is cheaper than shimming things later.

The reason for the 140mm externals is purely thermal driven, it'll allow us to put in 2 layers of insulation plus a air cavity which will greatly increase thermal efficiency and comfort.

I encoded the tolerances (no more than 3mm, over 4m spans and if out of tolerance delivery would be rejected) into the construction drawings and as a result the timber supplier provided us with everything *perfectly* straight and not cuped. Our carpentered who has 20+ years experience commented that he's impressed with the supply quality.

## Airtightness starts at the bottom

The join between the bottom plate and the slab is one of the biggest air leaks in a typical house, and it's almost impossible to fix once the walls are lined.

So I supplied the sole plate foam gaskets and sealants myself and put them in alongside the frame installer as each wall went down. The added benefit of working alongside the chippies was I could instantly answer their questions (and they'll a lot) and we made a few small tweaks along the way.

The roof is Colorbond, and I'm putting the Pro Clima roof wrap down myself under the sheeting. Like the gasket, it's a job I'd rather do slowly and carefully than have rushed.

The raked ceiling isn't up yet, they'll go in on Sat/Monday.

## An open frame (on purpose)

There's no structural sheathing on the walls, so the wrap has to work without a rigid backing. That ruled out self-adhered membranes like Pro Clima Adhero. We're using **Pro Clima Solitex Extasana** as the wall membrane, with **Foilboard** outside it. Pro Clima Mento will go on the roof.

This all goes on next.

## Windows aren't going in at this stage

The windows are here, but they're not going in just yet.

The plan is to wrap the Pro Clima and fuss over the details, getting everything perfectly taped and sealed. Foilboard will then go across the main areas of the walls and we'll leave a generous margin around every window opening. Then the windows will go in, getting taped properly, and the insulation is filled in around them.
